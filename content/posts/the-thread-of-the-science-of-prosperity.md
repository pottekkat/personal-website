The second book of the _The Nitopadesha_, _The Thread of the Science of Prosperity_ is a textbook of economics. It goes in to the intricacies of the market and talks about supply and demand, prices, opportunity costs, and even managing common goods.

As I had mentioned in the first part of this review/exploration of _The Nitopadesha_ it feels surreal to learn how wise and intelligent our ancestors were and the same time saddening to see how contemporary Indian politics and policymaking being stuck in wrong economics assumptions.

Perhaps our politicians and policymakers would be better at their jobs if they read _The Nitopadesha_ or if they at least took a minute to listen to what Bellikota, the cat had to say:

{{< blockquote title="The Story of the Generous Bear, The Nitopadesha" >}}
A price is right, just and fair\
Only when it reflects the scarcity of the resource,\
And truly counts all costs incurred\
And the opportunities foregone.
{{< /blockquote >}}

Without further ado, let's explore the lessons in this book. Unlike part 1, where I went in a bit more detail into the story in each chapter, I will focus only on the key takeaways. I would once again encourage everyone to read _The Nitopadesha_ and use these articles as a secondary tool to aid your reading and refer in the future.
