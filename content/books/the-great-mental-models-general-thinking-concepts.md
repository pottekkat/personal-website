_**The Great Mental Models**_ is a book series by Shane Parrish, the author of the Farnam Street blog. I have been a fan of the blog for a while and picked up hard copies of the series long ago. With everything that's going on, it feels like the right time to polish my understanding of the world and triple down as a high-agency generalist (I already doubled down).

Looking at the chapters, I thought I had a fairly good understanding of these concepts, since I have picked them up over the years and used them effectively. For example, thinking from first principles and second-order thinking come naturally to me, so I fail to see them as tools in my belt.

But this was a great read, and I had some surprises and aha moments throughout. The way to read such books is not to take notes or try to remember everything, but to think about how you are thinking through the book. I know it is very meta, but if you think deeply about your thinking as you read these books, it will be much more effective, and you'll be able to use these models in practice.

I also liked the anecdotes from the book. It really helps you solidify the idea. I also think it is best to read these books after you've lived through life a bit to fully appreciate them. For example, I'm much more interested in economics than I was in high school because I have been a part of the economic machine for a while and want to understand the world better.
