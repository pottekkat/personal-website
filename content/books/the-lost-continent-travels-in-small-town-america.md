_**The Lost Continent: Travels in Small-Town America**_ is a book I picked up for some reason, although for the life of me, I can't remember why.

Ever since Amazon started delivering to my small town in Kerala and I started making money, I have been impulsively buying books. So whenever I see someone recommend a book, I do a bit of research on it and then decide to buy it. But I do not read it immediately afterward. So when I pick up these books a year or more later, I do not remember why I wanted to read them.

So why did I get this book? Maybe I wanted to read more travelogues, maybe I wanted to read a funny book, or maybe it's my romanticization of small-town America and wanting to explore the continent myself.

I can make better use of the "Want to Read" status I have here, but maybe I'm too lazy. Regardless, I'm going to read this and see how it goes.

---

I loved the book's satirical, self-deprecating nature. But sometimes, I felt the author was coating his true feelings for America—which he surrenders to at the end of the book—with what Midwestern farmers on Goodreads are angry about.

Perhaps I'm reading it 40 years too late, and I'm looking at this retrospectively. Perhaps this was just a funny travel story.

When I look at the places in Google Maps now, they look very different. I know these small towns would have changed in the last 40 years, but I expected the suburbia of pre-2000 American TV shows and movies. Although I live on the other side of the world, I feel a certain fondness for it. It is something uniquely American.

Maybe I'm fascinated by the idea of the middle of nowhere because I'm from Kerala, which is one giant suburb. I have to go deep into the forests to find a population density similar to some of the places in the book.

I've also occasionally wondered, while reading the book, how difficult travel was back in the day. Especially going down these small roads with no other soul for miles. How do you even travel anymore without GPS? How do you trust a carburetor? Fuel injection is underappreciated.
