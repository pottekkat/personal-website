_**Blind Willow, Sleeping Woman**_ is a collection of stories that explores an entire range of human experiences. I was introduced to Murakami through his short story collection, "The Elephant Vanishes." While I have binged his novels since that rainy day at our local library, I haven't gone back to his short stories. At the beginning of the book, Murakami talks about how writing short stories is an entirely different feat from writing novels. I'm a couple of stories in already, and each of them feels like a Murakami novel condensed into a few pages.

I find a certain calmness when I read Murakami. Looking at some forums, I see many others feeling the same way. Each story in this collection has an intense but ephemeral feeling that I keep coming back to.

I started reading this on my Kindle almost a year ago, so it is hard not to be a bit biased by the later stories, but, in order, these are the stories I liked most: _The Kidney-Shaped Stone That Moves Every Day_, _A Shinagawa Monkey_, _Birthday Girl_, and _Tony Takitani_.
